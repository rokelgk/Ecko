# Ecko Tone: research and how it compares

## What GryzTone is

GryzTone / GRYZTONE PRO is sold by Gryzes Lab on Gumroad (about $35–39). Its own page
couldn't be fetched from the build environment, so this is from search listings. It is a
**Photoshop template, not a filter plugin**. You place your art in a smart object and get:

- a "live" halftone that can look like a Xerox copy or a classic dot screen,
- three textures prepared for printing,
- an overall vintage look aimed at shirt designers.

Gryzes Lab also sells a separate Photoshop action for halftones meant for DTF and DTG
printing. That tells us the audience: people making print-on-demand and DTF shirt designs.
No public tutorial videos turned up in searches. Similar templates (Medialoot Color
Halftone, WeGraphics Halftone, Envato duotone/Xerox templates, DEPTHTONE) work the same way.

## Where templates fall short, and what Ecko Tone does instead

| Template limitation | Ecko Tone |
|---|---|
| Needs Photoshop, and a smart-object workflow | Runs in a browser. There's also an optional Photoshop panel |
| One or two dot styles | 10 screens: round, classic (Euclidean), ellipse, square, diamond, line, cross, stochastic (FM), Xerox, mezzotint |
| Mono only, or a colour overlay | One ink, duotone, CMYK process, or 2–8 spot-colour separations |
| 3 fixed texture images | 6 procedural textures (worn, cracked, grunge, paper, scratches, toner) with strength, size and endless reseeded variations, plus your own texture image |
| Halftone size tied to document pixels | Real LPI, angle, DPI and print size, with tone accuracy checked (a 30% grey prints at about 30% coverage) |
| Soft, anti-aliased edges: semi-transparent pixels that DTF RIPs print as a white haze or drop | Transparency is strictly on/off in every export |
| No press control | Minimum and maximum dot, dot-gain compensation, rough edges, misregistration |
| No feedback | Print check: tone levels, smallest dot in mm compared with the process, LPI range, heavy-film warning, ink matching the garment, with one-click fixes |
| Made for one shirt colour | Fit to shirt recolours ink or re-solves tones for any shirt, drops spot inks the shirt supplies, and adds an underbase on dark shirts |
| One design at a time | Batch: many designs × many shirt colours, with mockups |
| Zoomed-out preview | A 1:1 loupe shows the exact print pixels, the same as the export |
| Screen printers do separations by hand | ZIP of 1-bit films with registration marks and a README of inks and angles |

The printer limits used in the checks (DTF 25–45 LPI with dots of about 0.25 mm or more,
screen print 35–65 LPI, and so on) are common rules of thumb. Calibrate them on your own
press.
